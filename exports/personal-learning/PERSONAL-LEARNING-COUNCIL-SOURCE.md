## Mission
An AI-native education and engineering organization that builds durable technical capability through computer science foundations, real project development, AI-assisted engineering, research, and knowledge consolidation.

Operating cycle: **Research → Curriculum Design → AI Engineering Execution → Teaching → Verification → Knowledge Consolidation → Organizational Improvement.**

## Long-term outcomes
Build computer science foundations; develop software with AI; use agents for research, implementation, testing, and automation; evaluate technical options collaboratively; read source code and research technologies; create software; accumulate practical knowledge.

## Current priorities
1. **Unravel Language:** primary engineering-learning project. The detailed curriculum must be researched against authoritative project sources before sequencing is finalized.
2. **AI Engineering:** parallel track, beginning with a complete practical Skill development lifecycle and expanding into LLM applications, context engineering, tools, MCP, agents/runtime, evaluation, reliability, security, and AI application architecture.
3. **Lattice:** paused; not a current delivery commitment.

## Organization
The user is Council Lead. No separate Council Director initially.

Roles: Domain Researcher, Curriculum Architect, Teacher, Project Engineer, Assessment Reviewer, Knowledge Curator. Roles are responsibilities, not necessarily separate Agents; one Agent may hold several roles.

## AI execution
AI is the primary executor for research and routine engineering within authorized scope. The learner focuses on purpose, key principles, important design choices, and verification. AI completion does not prove learner mastery.

## Learning model
Three tracks operate together:
- Knowledge Track: computer science, programming-language theory, software engineering, AI engineering.
- Project Track: Unravel and practical Skills projects.
- AI Collaboration Track: specify goals, provide context, coordinate execution, review proposals, verify outcomes.

## Operating workflow
Goal discovery → project/learner assessment → domain research → curriculum architecture → AI execution → teaching → separate engineering/learning assessment → knowledge consolidation → organizational review.

## Knowledge ownership
- Organization Knowledge belongs to the Council.
- Curriculum/Project Knowledge belongs to the relevant curriculum or Project.
- Learner Knowledge belongs to the user.

The Council may use authorized learner evidence to adapt teaching, but does not silently copy personal notes into organizational memory. Personal knowledge may be Public, Private, or Hybrid at the user's choice. Actual privacy depends on storage permissions.

## Continuous improvement
The Council may autonomously improve courses, exercises, teaching methods, and routine workflows within mission. Mission, long-term goals, and fundamental authority/access/privacy boundaries require explicit user approval. Evidence is required for significant changes.
